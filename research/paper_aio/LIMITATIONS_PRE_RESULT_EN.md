# Pre-Result Limitations Statement

Status: pre-result, protocol-bound draft. This document contains no empirical performance claim and cannot authorize selection of a method, checkpoint, or narrative branch.

The first full-data study uses one prespecified training seed (`2026`) to resolve long-horizon behavior under a fixed compute budget. A positive result in this wave therefore does not establish seed robustness. Additional seeds may be run only after the algorithm, claims, and primary table are frozen; they cannot be used to revise the method retrospectively.

All methods share the same image exposure, update count, resolution, and data-epoch definition, but they do not necessarily use equal floating-point work. In particular, conditional multi-view estimators and all-player corrections may require more forward or backward passes than plain UNSB. We will report parameters, peak memory, wall time per update or epoch, view count, and inference NFE where applicable. Timing measured on different hosts or under different co-residency conditions will not be presented as an intrinsic algorithmic comparison.

The theoretical guarantees for Proposal-only, ST-CGR, and AM-TNC are deliberately local. Their pre-Adam conditional averaging and consensus statements concern the estimator before the optimizer update. They do not imply equality of optimizer displacements, equality of the induced parameter-space Markov kernels, convergence of the non-convex adversarial game, or improvement in PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, KID, or FID. A reduction in conditional gradient variance is a mechanism claim, not a restoration-quality theorem.

The current methods do not alter the terminal bridge law and are not presented as a repair for terminal-step singularities or vanishing-variance pathologies. The low-variance and spectral analyses are diagnostic: they can identify associations among bridge coordinates, perturbation amplification, and later behavior, but they do not by themselves establish causality or a convergence guarantee.

The main experiment is a single All-in-One, unpaired, six-domain benchmark at 128 x 128 resolution. Training follows the frozen image-proportional marginal, which preserves the natural domain imbalance rather than balancing domains. Results therefore characterize this specific mixture, exposure rule, architecture, and horizon; they should not be generalized to other degradations, resolutions, datasets, or paired restoration without additional evidence.

The benchmark is assembled from datasets with their own licenses and redistribution conditions. Code, manifests, hashes, and lawful metadata can be released, but source images will not be redistributed where the original license does not permit it. Any missing public artifact must be recorded as a lawful omission rather than silently replaced.

DDSB remains a reproduction gap unless an authoritative formula-to-source mapping and the frozen implementation gate are completed. Its absence cannot be described as a negative result. CUT, CycleGAN, and DCLGAN use method-appropriate optimization protocols and source-bound exports; they are external baselines, not members of the matched UNSB runtime cohort. Deferred HJCGR and NEGCUT experiments likewise must not be described as mechanism falsification.

The `confirmation20` split remains sealed until the algorithm set, claims, and primary result table have been committed. It may then be opened once for confirmation and cannot be used to tune, select, stop, or rewrite the method. Primary reporting uses the frozen epoch-200 checkpoint, with epochs 150, 175, and 200 used for the sustained criterion; no best-checkpoint result is allowed.

One long-running AM-TNC process survived deletion of its original Python environment because the executable and mapped dependencies remained resident in the operating system. Recovery readiness was established in an isolated, hash-audited runtime and against a full-state checkpoint without interrupting the healthy process. This protects recoverability, but it cannot prove bitwise equality of the next CUDA update between the still-running deleted-inode process and a newly launched process. Any actual recovery event will therefore be disclosed as an engineering provenance boundary and evaluated only through the prespecified protocol.

Finally, this study separates three outcomes that are often conflated: an engineering or reproduction failure, failure of a particular implementation under the current protocol, and falsification of a mathematical mechanism. Only the evidence actually obtained under the frozen protocol can support one of these labels. Resource deferral, unavailable source code, or a failed runtime-equivalence gate is not scientific evidence against a mechanism.
